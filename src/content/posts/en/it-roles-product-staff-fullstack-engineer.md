You read a **Product Engineer** job listing asking for React, Node.js and PostgreSQL. You open another for a **Full-Stack Engineer**, and it has almost the same stack. Then you find **Staff Engineer**, but the description mentions architecture, customers and coordination across teams. Are these different jobs, or different names for the same work?

Some of the confusion comes from mixing **technical specialty, the focus of the work, responsibility level and people management**. These concepts can coexist in a title: someone can be a _Senior Full-Stack Product Engineer_ without any of those words being redundant.

To prepare this article, I reviewed job descriptions published by companies hiring in Europe and the United States, alongside public career frameworks. The review took place on **October 13, 2026**. This is a deliberate selection focused on product companies and software providers. It helps compare responsibilities; it cannot measure market demand. To add that context, I include statistics from Eurostat, Indeed Hiring Lab and the BLS later on, with their time periods and limitations. Some listings may change or close.

The focus is software engineering and roles close to product, infrastructure, data and security. IT also includes support, systems administration, networking and other professions that deserve their own explanation.

## Four questions to make sense of an engineering title

Before comparing salaries or deciding whether you fit, separate these questions:

<div class="overflow-x-auto" role="region" aria-label="Dimensions of engineering job titles" tabindex="0">

| Dimension                 | Question                                                | Examples                                              |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| Specialty                 | Which part of the system will you work on?              | Frontend, Backend, Full-Stack, Data, Platform.        |
| Focus                     | Which problem or audience does your work address?       | Product, reliability, security, customer integration. |
| Level                     | What autonomy, complexity and influence do they expect? | Junior, Mid-Level, Senior, Staff, Principal.          |
| Leadership responsibility | Do you lead technical decisions, people or both?        | Tech Lead, Engineering Manager.                       |

</div>

**Full-Stack and Staff are not alternative categories.** One describes technical scope; the other usually describes a career level. GitLab, for example, publishes a specific framework for [Staff Fullstack Engineer](https://handbook.gitlab.com/handbook/marketing/marketing-career-development/contributor-success/staff/).

There is no universal equivalence between titles at different companies, either. The description, team and expected outcomes tell you much more than the name.

## Software Engineer, Frontend, Backend and Full-Stack: where you build

**Software Engineer** is a broad title. It can refer to developing applications, internal systems, tools or infrastructure. To understand it, you need to read about the team's domain: payments, identity, data or user experience, for example.

### Frontend Engineer: the interface is a system too

A **Frontend Engineer** focuses on the part of the product that users interact with. The work can include components, navigation, state, API integration, performance and accessibility. Reducing it to “laying out screens” hides much of the job.

The [Senior Frontend Engineer listing at Synthesia for London and Europe](https://jobs.ashbyhq.com/synthesia/46d13eb9-9d94-435b-a141-19524b9e5c34) includes ownership of the component library, content model and rendering system for its website. It also includes accessibility in the definition of done.

The domain matters: building a collaborative editor, a financial dashboard or a marketing website requires different decisions, even if all three use React.

### Backend Engineer: services, data and integrations

A **Backend Engineer** usually works on services, APIs, business rules, persistence and integrations. The role can also involve concurrency, asynchronous processing, observability and production operations.

In Europe, [Flip is hiring a Product Engineer specializing in Backend](https://jobs.ashbyhq.com/flip/afac1428-c910-427d-90a4-4589f0450232). The listing asks for Kotlin or Java, experience with SaaS systems and ownership of APIs, data models and infrastructure, while staying close to the user journey.

A useful question here is who owns the service after deployment. “Backend” alone does not tell you whether there is on-call work, infrastructure management or another team sharing those tasks.

### Full-Stack Engineer: taking a feature from end to end

A **Full-Stack Engineer** works on both frontend and backend. They can deliver a feature spanning the interface, API and database, keeping those parts consistent.

The expected depth varies between listings. [Maze advertises a Product Engineer (Full Stack) role for Europe](https://jobs.ashbyhq.com/mazehq/971941ec-5aa2-49eb-bb0d-fed342455619), asking for balanced frontend and backend proficiency, expressed as 50/50 in the requirements. That describes the expected capabilities; it does not guarantee how your working day will be divided. Its stack combines React and TypeScript with Python and FastAPI.

I would check what _full-stack_ means to that team. It might include mobile, deployments or infrastructure; at another company, it might be limited to the web application. I would also ask where they expect your greatest strength.

## Product Engineer: helping decide what is worth building

A **Product Engineer** actively participates in understanding the user's problem, choosing a solution, implementing it and checking what happens after launch.

The emphasis is ownership of the product outcome. The role can be full-stack, frontend or another specialty.

The [Product Engineer listing at Squad in New York](https://jobs.ashbyhq.com/squadai/e4e8e9ec-b4a2-45ff-86af-52e91cbaf143) includes frequent conversations with users and involvement in defining what to build. It asks for full-stack experience, but also judgment about the customer's problem.

Maze's listing combines product and Full Stack in the title. Flip's combines product and Backend. These are two European examples of why Product Engineer does not require a single technical specialty.

### Product Engineer versus Full-Stack Engineer

The useful distinction is **which responsibility the company makes explicit**:

- **Full-Stack:** you can implement a solution across multiple layers.
- **Product Engineer:** you are expected to help understand the problem, question proposals and assess the outcome.
- **Full-Stack Product Engineer:** both responsibilities are present.

A Full-Stack Engineer can also have strong product judgment. A Backend Engineer can spot that a feature does not solve the customer's problem. These capabilities are not exclusive to a title.

Imagine many users abandon a form. One possible technical task is to implement autosave. With product responsibility, you would also investigate when they leave, whether you ask for information too early and whether a shorter form would solve the problem better.

This example is hypothetical. The point is that the initial solution can change once you understand the need.

### Product Engineer versus Product Manager

A **Product Manager (PM)** usually coordinates product strategy, prioritization and goals with design, engineering and other functions. The Product Engineer participates in those conversations and also builds and maintains the technical solution.

The [Group Product Manager listing at Volta in Palo Alto](https://jobs.ashbyhq.com/volta/5e53f6f0-d3e2-43f3-9591-14af3fbb77ea) assigns strategy, roadmap, prioritization and efficiency metrics to the role. It is an example of product management in a technical domain.

Engineering autonomy does not mean replacing product or design. Collaboration and specific decisions depend on the organization.

## Junior, Mid-Level and Senior: autonomy before years

Years of experience help put an application in context, but they do not describe a level by themselves. To interpret these labels, I would look at three things: **which problems you can solve, how much help you need and which outcomes you own**.

As a guide to reading a listing, rather than a universal scale:

<div class="overflow-x-auto" role="region" aria-label="How to interpret experience levels" tabindex="0">

| Level                    | Responsibility to look for in the listing                                                                                 |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Junior / Entry-Level     | Clearly scoped work, guidance, review and learning the environment.                                                       |
| Mid-Level / Intermediate | Independently delivers defined work; identifies risks and asks for help when appropriate.                                 |
| Senior                   | Solves less clearly defined problems, evaluates alternatives and supports others, considering maintenance and operations. |

</div>

[GitLab's Senior infrastructure framework](https://handbook.gitlab.com/handbook/engineering/careers/matrix/infrastructure/senior/) includes mentoring, process improvement and safe operations. It is a concrete reference for how a level covers more than writing code.

When a listing requires “five years,” read it as that company's requirement. It does not automatically make everyone with that experience Senior, or let you compare its levels with another organization's.

## Staff Engineer and Principal Engineer: technical leadership with varying scope

This is often where another confusion begins: assuming that the only way to advance beyond Senior is to manage people.

**Individual Contributor (IC)** means you are on the technical contribution track. It does not mean working alone or having no leadership responsibilities. GitLab describes [IC Leadership as an alternative to Engineering Management](https://handbook.gitlab.com/handbook/engineering/careers/ic-leadership/), where communication, collaboration and developing others are part of the work.

### Staff Engineer: expanding technical impact

A **Staff Engineer** usually tackles ambiguous or high-impact problems, guides technical decisions and helps other engineers resolve them. They may write code, design a migration, unblock dependencies or help reach an architectural agreement.

The scope varies by company:

- [Curri in the United States](https://jobs.ashbyhq.com/curri/d616afe4-311f-4c73-8dc9-87c40f8c7ea8) describes work across multiple projects and teams, architecture review and mentoring, while maintaining direct code contributions.
- [GitLab's Staff framework](https://handbook.gitlab.com/handbook/engineering/careers/matrix/staff/) generally places the scope within a team, with technical leadership over one or more of its domains.

“Staff always works across multiple teams” would therefore be too rigid a definition. Ask which domain they will own and which decisions they are expected to help resolve.

### Principal Engineer: broader technical direction

In organizations that distinguish the two levels, a **Principal Engineer** usually has broader scope than Staff. The work can include technical strategy, long-term decisions and coordination across areas.

The [Principal Engineer listing at Omaze in London](https://jobs.ashbyhq.com/omaze/ea40744f-b954-4f24-a410-469944cb346d) describes an IC collaborating with the CTO and engineering and product teams, including technical research and prototypes. [GitLab's Principal framework](https://handbook.gitlab.com/job-description-library/engineering/development/management/principal-engineer/) describes multiple teams and the scope of a department or subdepartment.

You may also see levels such as **Senior Staff** or **Distinguished Engineer**. To interpret them, you need that company's career framework; placing them in a universal table based on years is not useful.

## Tech Lead and Engineering Manager: what you lead and who you manage

A **Tech Lead** guides the technical execution of a team or initiative: design decisions, risks, work coordination and support for other engineers. It can be a responsibility taken on by a Senior or Staff Engineer, or a formal position.

An **Engineering Manager (EM)** owns people management and how the team functions. Check whether that includes hiring, professional development, performance reviews, capacity and delivery coordination.

[Ashby's Engineering Manager listing for Europe](https://jobs.ashbyhq.com/ashby/7458d4e9-da2e-47bd-98cb-adfda43d42b2) focuses on individual performance, processes and culture, leaving engineers considerable room to make technical and product decisions.

To distinguish the roles in an interview, I would ask:

> Will I have direct reports? Who will handle their performance reviews and hiring decisions? Which technical decisions are expected of me?

**Lead Engineer** and **Team Lead** also need that clarification. [Volta is hiring a Platform Engineering Team Lead in London](https://jobs.ashbyhq.com/volta/ee30f481-97b7-4ac9-9df4-7b05dc4e2273), combining technical direction and coding with performance discussions, interviews and team support. Some companies bring both responsibilities together in one role.

## CTO: connecting technology strategy with the business

**CTO** stands for _Chief Technology Officer_. It is an executive role that guides how technology supports the company's goals, working with the CEO and other functions. Its scope can include product, architecture, teams, infrastructure, data and technology risks.

Responsibilities may include:

- Defining the technology vision and long-term priorities, considering customer and business needs.
- Contributing to investment, capacity and hiring decisions; weighing costs, vendors and build-versus-buy options.
- Guiding technical standards and decisions about architecture, quality and security, distributing ownership across teams.

[Unipro's CTO listing in the United Kingdom](https://www.unipro.io/career/chief-technology-officer-cto/) combines strategy aligned with commercial goals, architecture, team development and delivery oversight. It also includes advising customers and leading its AI offering. This is an example of how the company's domain shapes a CTO's work.

**Organization size changes the day-to-day work considerably.** At a small startup, the CTO may design and develop the product directly. At a larger company, they may focus more on strategy and coordination with other leaders, delegating much of the implementation and day-to-day management.

The useful distinction is scope: a **Tech Lead** guides technical execution within a team or initiative; an **Engineering Manager** focuses on people and how the team functions; a **CTO** connects the company's technology direction with its business decisions. In small teams, one person may take on all three responsibilities.

When reading a CTO listing, I would ask: **which decisions will I own, with what team and budget, and how much hands-on development is expected?** The title alone does not answer those questions.

## DevOps, Platform Engineer and SRE: similar tools, different goals

Seeing Kubernetes, Terraform and observability in three listings does not guarantee that the jobs are equivalent.

### DevOps Engineer: delivery and infrastructure automation

DevOps is a way of organizing collaboration between development and operations, and also a hiring label. Under **DevOps Engineer**, you may find Infrastructure as Code (**IaC**), continuous integration and continuous delivery (**CI/CD**) pipelines and operational automation.

The [DevOps Engineer listing at Ema in the United States](https://jobs.ashbyhq.com/ema/6f816cca-bf17-4061-b751-033a14e433cb) combines Kubernetes, Terraform, Ansible, deployments and observability. It also accepts previous experience in DevOps, SRE or Platform, a sign of the overlap.

### Platform Engineer: building tools for other teams

A **Platform Engineer** usually builds reusable capabilities that help other developers or researchers work: environments, deployments, automation, self-service interfaces and documentation.

[Mistral is hiring a Research Platform Engineer for Paris, London and Warsaw](https://jobs.ashbyhq.com/mistral.ai/5d444fcb-4101-44a7-9996-45819443d640). Its internal users are researchers, and the work includes development environments, research CI and build optimization.

To understand this listing, I would ask who uses the platform and how the team measures whether those tools help them.

### Site Reliability Engineer: reliability as engineering work

A **Site Reliability Engineer (SRE)** focuses on keeping services reliable through engineering and automation. In [Google's introduction to SRE](https://sre.google/sre-book/introduction/), reducing repetitive operational work is part of the approach.

The [Site Reliability Engineer listing at Mistral Cloud](https://jobs.ashbyhq.com/mistral.ai/4b25c97e-ab58-4148-9126-9d4e2c41aaf7), with European locations such as Paris, Amsterdam and Warsaw, includes production operations, monitoring, incident response and on-call rotations. Other descriptions also include **Service Level Objectives (SLOs)**: ask which reliability commitments the team uses.

For any of these roles, I would check on-call duties, incident frequency and the time available for automation. Those conditions make a big difference to daily work.

## Data and AI: Data Engineer, ML Engineer and AI Engineer

A **Data Engineer** moves, transforms and serves data at a quality level suitable for its consumers. The role can involve building pipelines, data models and platforms for analytics or applications.

The US [Data Engineer listing at Ohr](https://jobs.ashbyhq.com/ohr/5f2b1267-f692-4be6-b796-bd7e8cf53ee3) connects laboratory platforms and instruments and builds a shared foundation for analytics and AI systems.

A **Machine Learning Engineer (ML Engineer)** usually works on systems that train, evaluate, deploy or serve models. The balance between those tasks depends on the team. [Trace in the United States](https://jobs.ashbyhq.com/tracelabs/84f1786a-1f06-4798-892c-682a6ab07ba5) asks for ownership of data, training, evaluation and deployment.

An **AI Engineer**, especially in listings for applications using **Large Language Models (LLMs)**, may focus on integrating models and building products: information retrieval, RAG, tools, agents and evaluation. [Eloquent AI in London](https://jobs.ashbyhq.com/eloquentai/b636810c-e01b-4f9b-8aed-9a0856fee42e) combines full-stack development with LLM integration, RAG and automation in production applications.

You will also see **Data Scientist**, associated with analysis, experimentation and modeling to answer questions. The boundary with ML Engineer varies between companies; checking whether both ask for Python is not enough.

When reading an AI listing, I would look for a concrete answer: **will I train models, operate inference, integrate existing models or do some combination of these?**

### LLM Engineer and Agent Engineer: from a model to a useful task

An LLM Engineer can integrate an existing model into an application; they do not necessarily train it from scratch. **RAG** (_Retrieval-Augmented Generation_) supplies retrieved information to generate a response. An agent adds tool use and task execution, introducing decisions about permissions, failures and oversight.

Imagine an invoice assistant: answering a question about an invoice and authorizing its payment have different consequences. The title Agent Engineer does not tell you which actions the system can take or who defines those boundaries. I would ask how they evaluate outcomes, when a person intervenes and who owns production errors.

### Research, platform, evaluation and safety: overlapping boundaries

**Research Engineer does not mean only implementing other people's ideas**, and Scientist does not mean spending all day writing research papers. OpenAI's listings distinguish a research agenda from building systems, but both require experimentation and technical contributions.

**MLOps** (_Machine Learning Operations_) and **LLMOps** describe practices for operating models and LLM applications. They can sit within ML Platform, infrastructure or a product team. They are not automatically equivalent to DevOps, nor do they guarantee a separate role.

**Evaluation** measures quality and reliability; **AI Safety** studies and reduces risks; **red teaming** looks for failures through adversarial tests. They may collaborate, but they are not synonyms. Mistral's listing focuses on evaluation infrastructure; Cohere's adds data and post-training algorithms for agents that act on external resources. Access security still requires security engineering alongside model evaluation.

### Prompt Engineer and Context Engineer: instructions versus available information

Working with prompts can be an AI Engineer's responsibility or a specific role, as at MCI. The work includes testing variations and measuring outcomes, not just writing instructions.

_Context engineering_ broadens the question to **which information, state and tools the model receives**. Capgemini's listing places it close to data and knowledge engineering; OpenAI includes context construction within Applied AI. I would not assume that all Context Engineers work on the same things.

**RAG and MCP** (_Model Context Protocol_) are techniques or technologies that can appear in these jobs, not standardized professional categories. Before changing your title, check what they expect you to deliver: a product, a model, a platform, evaluations or AI-ready data.

## Other titles worth recognizing

These roles add another dimension to the map:

- **QA Engineer / Test Automation Engineer / SDET:** quality, test design and, depending on the listing, building automation tools. SDET stands for _Software Development Engineer in Test_. [Almedia is hiring a QA Lead in Berlin](https://jobs.ashbyhq.com/almedia/83936f61-954c-4599-a654-79fc55dd3323), with responsibility for test strategy, automation and product quality. Clarify the balance between exploratory work, automation and tool maintenance.
- **Security Engineer / Application Security Engineer:** controls and risk reduction in applications, infrastructure or internal systems. [Volta advertises a Security Engineer, Compliance Focus role for Palo Alto and London](https://jobs.ashbyhq.com/volta/67c9c08a-368e-4f68-9a3c-6f3aa05e9f2b), including implementing and documenting controls and preparing audits. The qualifier helps identify the domain; this example focuses on compliance and does not define all security engineering.
- **Solutions Engineer:** may work close to sales on demonstrations, technical discovery and proofs of concept. Ashby's US [Enterprise Solutions Engineering Manager listing](https://jobs.ashbyhq.com/ashby/dfc35159-ffe5-4d42-bea3-552b5dc17628) describes those tasks within the team the manager will lead. Do not assume the Solutions Engineer's main job will be maintaining the product.
- **Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE):** works close to the customer to bring a solution into their environment and into production. [Mistral's Applied AI, Forward Deployed Engineer listing in Europe](https://jobs.ashbyhq.com/mistral.ai/647be1ec-19d1-421c-9c49-6bbf12c0c93a) focuses on building use cases on its stack. Ask about travel, customer-specific projects and ongoing ownership of integrations.
- **Founding Engineer:** describes joining at an early stage. [Marloo's listing in London](https://jobs.ashbyhq.com/marloo/50fa9b89-69fd-436b-af88-6d6e902e6973) includes full-stack development, architectural decisions and direct collaboration with the founders. The title alone does not establish that you are a co-founder or have a particular equity stake: check the agreement.
- **Software Architect / Solutions Architect:** check whether architecture refers to the internal product, customer solutions or organizational standards. Ask how much implementation work it includes and what decision-making authority the role has.

I would not assume that _Member of Technical Staff (MTS)_ is equivalent to _Staff Engineer_. [Callosum uses Applied AI - Member of Technical Staff](https://jobs.ashbyhq.com/callosum/e33ede97-fee9-4c0a-8063-9944923fa36b) for a role focused on customer systems and integrations. You need to understand its internal leveling.

## IT job demand: what the data says and what we can compare

So far, we have compared responsibilities. To discuss demand, we need to distinguish **published job advertisements**, **unfilled vacancies** and **projected employment**. One advertisement can represent several positions, appear more than once or never lead to a hire. A projection describes a future scenario; it does not count jobs available today.

The following figures were retrieved on **October 13, 2026**. Each section uses its own time period and classification. I have not turned the results into a ranking of Product, Full-Stack or Staff roles: the sources do not separate those titles in a comparable way.

To read them, first check **what is being counted, in which country, over which period and in which unit**. A longer bar can mean more advertisements or a higher growth percentage, depending on the chart. Comparing bar lengths across different charts is not useful.

In Eurostat's annual series, **2024 is the latest year available** at the time of the query. The data records approximately **633,690 online advertisements for ICT specialists** across the covered portals. **What the parameters mean.** `total postings` covers all postings present on Indeed, whereas `new postings` includes only those that are seven days old or less. The **index** sets each category's level on February 1, 2020 to 100: 120 means 20% more than then, and 80 means 20% less. Those are not 120 or 80 advertisements.

Seasonal adjustment attempts to remove patterns that repeat throughout the year; the seven-day moving average smooths daily fluctuations using that day and the previous six.

**How to read the chart.** The horizontal axis shows percentages, not the original index. Each category has two bars: **blue** compares October 2026 with October 2025; **orange** compares it with February 2020. To the right of zero is an increase relative to that reference; to the left is a decline. The two bars use different baselines: their percentages should not be added together.

**An example: software development.** Its index rises from **64.30 to 78.33** between October 2025 and October 2026. Applying `(78.33 / 64.30 - 1) * 100` gives growth of **21.8%**. But 78.33 is still below the 2020 baseline of 100, which explains the **−21.7%** in the other column. Both results are compatible: postings recover from the previous year without yet returning to their 2020 level.

All four categories show that recovery, with software growing the most within this selection. Saying only “software is up 21.8%” would hide its distance from 2020.

The index lets us compare changes, not absolute posting counts between categories. It also does not measure hires, establish that employment is growing or let us attribute the change to AI.

I would use the data to understand the context of an occupational family, then investigate the market in the country, sector and level that interest me. **Higher percentage growth does not mean more listings in absolute terms**, and a favorable projection does not guarantee an easy job search today.

## One problem, several responsibilities

Imagine a store whose users abandon checkout. This allocation is hypothetical; several people may share the responsibilities:

- The **Product Engineer** investigates the problem with product and design, proposes an intervention and checks its outcome.
- The **Frontend Engineer** improves validation, accessibility and error recovery in the interface.
- The **Backend Engineer** reviews order state, payment integration and retries.
- The **Full-Stack Engineer** can implement changes spanning those layers.
- The **Staff Engineer** can help resolve inconsistencies across teams if the problem affects several parts of the system.
- The **Tech Lead** coordinates the initiative's decisions and risks.
- The **Engineering Manager** checks that the team has capacity, support and a sustainable workload.

One person can take on more than one of those responsibilities. To assess a listing, you need to know which would be yours and what support you would have.

## Questions to discover the work behind the listing

I would bring these questions to the first conversation:

1. **What outcome do you expect in the first three months?** Ask for an example of a problem, rather than just a list of technologies.
2. **Who decides what to build?** Clarify your involvement with product, design and customers.
3. **What does this level mean in your career framework?** Ask about autonomy, scope and evaluation criteria.
4. **Will I have direct reports?** Distinguish mentoring from performance reviews and formal management.
5. **Who operates what we build?** Check on-call duties, incidents and maintenance ownership.
6. **How is the work divided?** Features, technical debt, integrations, infrastructure and meetings.
7. **What geographic conditions apply to remote work?** Countries, working hours, travel, contract and eligibility.

You do not need every answer to fit a textbook definition. You need them to be clear and consistent with one another.

## Frequently asked questions about engineering roles

### Is Product Engineer just another name for Full-Stack Engineer?

There is a lot of overlap. Full-Stack makes technical breadth explicit; Product Engineer emphasizes involvement in the problem and outcome. Maze combines them in the same title.

### Does a Staff Engineer have direct reports?

The title alone does not guarantee it. Technical leadership and mentoring can be part of the IC track. Formal people management needs to be clarified separately.

### Is Staff above Senior at every company?

It is commonly a later level, but you need the internal framework to compare. An equivalence based only on the title can be misleading.

### Do I have to become a manager to keep growing?

Some organizations have separate technical and management tracks, such as GitLab. Check which options exist at the company that interests you.

Before sending your next application, turn the listing into four lines: **specialty, the problem you will solve, scope of responsibility and people management**. Then compare them with the work you want to do. That reading will give you better questions than the title alone.
